# Bad Cases

## Purpose
Record recurring output failures so they can be corrected systematically.

## Bad Case 1 — Overly generic proposal
### Problem
The proposal sounds impressive but does not identify a specific workflow or scope.

### Why it is bad
- client cannot understand the deliverable
- scope becomes ambiguous
- increases revision risk

### Fix
Force proposal generation to name one workflow and one clear next step.

---

## Bad Case 2 — Workflow map without manual review boundaries
### Problem
The workflow suggests automation but does not specify what remains human-controlled.

### Why it is bad
- creates unrealistic expectations
- weakens trust
- unsafe for sensitive cases

### Fix
Always include human-in-the-loop steps.

---

## Bad Case 3 — Discovery summary invents details
### Problem
The output adds tools, channels, or operational details not present in the source notes.

### Why it is bad
- reduces trust
- contaminates later proposals
- creates incorrect assumptions

### Fix
Use explicit missing-information sections instead of invented facts.

---

## Bad Case 4 — Outreach message sounds like spam
### Problem
The outreach is too long, too sales-heavy, or too vague.

### Why it is bad
- lower response rate
- sounds mass-produced
- hides the real value proposition

### Fix
Keep outreach short, pain-focused, and centered on one workflow.

---

## Bad Case 5 — Delivery pack is too internal
### Problem
The final document includes internal terminology, prompt language, or implementation detail the client does not need.

### Why it is bad
- makes delivery look unfinished
- confuses the client
- reduces perceived professionalism

### Fix
Rewrite delivery docs for business readability only.
